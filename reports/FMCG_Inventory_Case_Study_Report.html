1. Executive Summary & Business Problem

In fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) distribution operations across Indonesia, maintaining high customer service level while minimizing inventory holding costs is critical. This project evaluates 180 days of replenishment and outbound fulfillment data (13,500 order transactions) across 3 regional distribution centers: DC Cakung (Greater Jakarta Hub), DC Rungkut (East Java Hub), and DC KIM Medan (Sumatra Hub) across 25 high-velocity SKUs spanning Food & Beverage, Fabric Care, Personal Care, and Home Care.

The Core Challenge: The baseline distribution network operates under static min/max replenishment policies. As a consequence, high-velocity SKUs suffered severe stockouts during demand spikes (causing Rp 5.42 Billion in lost gross revenue and pulling network OTIF down to 84.2%, far below the FMCG industry gold standard of 95%). Concurrently, slow-moving SKUs locked up excess working capital in regional warehouses.

4. Mathematical Optimization Model (Safety Stock, ROP, EOQ)

The dynamic inventory engine replaces static days-of-supply with dual-uncertainty statistical formulas that incorporate both daily demand variance (σD) and supplier lead time variability (σL) at 95% service level factor (Z = 1.65):

1. Dynamic Safety Stock (SS): SS = Z × √[ L × (σD)² + D² × (σL)² ]
2. Dynamic Reorder Point (ROP): ROP = (Average Daily Demand × Average Lead Time) + Safety Stock
3. Economic Order Quantity (EOQ): EOQ = √[ (2 × Annual Demand × Ordering Cost) / Holding Cost ]
